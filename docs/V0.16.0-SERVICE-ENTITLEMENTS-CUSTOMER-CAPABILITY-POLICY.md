# V0.16.0 — Service Entitlements + Customer Capability Access Policy

## Purpose

V0.16.0 implements the authorization-policy foundation required before the separate Customer Workspace may expose any NEXT F-managed website capability to a customer.

The governing rule is the approved most-restrictive intersection:

```text
Canonical Contract maximum
∩ validated Site Manifest support
∩ Workspace/service entitlement
∩ active Customer Workspace membership
∩ customer-role exact canonical permission
∩ resource lifecycle/state
∩ field-level policy
∩ privacy/security restriction
= effective customer capability
```

The most restrictive result wins. A missing or stale customer-write policy fails closed.

This release does **not** implement the Customer Workspace frontend or its public/customer API. It implements the staff-side policy records and a reusable authorization evaluator that the later trusted API boundary must enforce server-side.

## Source constraint carried forward from V0.15

The development environment still does not contain the authoritative Contract Registry `1.0.0` snapshot, Registry root files/starters, or a real managed-site `nextf.site.json`, and `https://contracts.nextf.lk/` is not reachable from this execution environment.

Therefore V0.16.0 does not seed or invent any Module, Capability, field, Permission, API-operation, event, webhook, integration, Admin-profile or Customer-CMS-profile ID.

The new policy system remains empty until official V0.15 validation evidence is followed by an official resolver-derived **Canonical Customer Access Projection** for the exact Site validation evidence.

## Canonical Customer Access Projection

`customerCapabilityPolicyStore.ts` introduces a cached resolver projection containing only customer-authorization metadata required by the CMS policy layer:

- canonical Capability ID,
- optional canonical Module ID,
- maximum customer exposure,
- maximum customer publishing mode,
- exact canonical permission requirements for read/edit/publish,
- canonical fields,
- per-field maximum exposure,
- exact field read/edit permission requirements.

A projection can be recorded only when:

1. the Site Connection exists;
2. the Site has canonical V0.15 validation evidence;
3. the exact Registry snapshot is trusted;
4. the Site manifest status is `valid`;
5. the Site Connection is `ready` or `connected`;
6. the Contract Version matches the Site, validation evidence and Registry snapshot;
7. the verification authority matches trusted evidence;
8. every projected Capability ID is present in V0.15 canonical resolution;
9. every projected Module ID is present in V0.15 canonical resolution;
10. every projected Permission ID is present in V0.15 canonical resolution.

Duplicate Capability IDs and duplicate/empty canonical field paths are rejected.

Canonical access projections are retained as immutable history. Effective authorization selects only the projection matching the Site Connection's **current** `validationEvidenceId`. If the Site is revalidated, an older access projection becomes stale automatically and customer authorization fails closed until the official resolver supplies a new matching projection.

## Service capability entitlements

A service entitlement is deliberately separate from a Digital service, project, workspace membership and Site capability.

An entitlement references:

- Customer Workspace,
- managed Site Connection,
- existing NEXT F Digital service,
- canonical Capability ID,
- lifecycle state,
- start/end dates,
- staff actor/audit evidence.

An entitlement may be created only when:

- the Digital service exists and is active;
- the Site belongs to that Customer Workspace;
- the Site has valid canonical Contract evidence;
- the canonical capability exists in the trusted customer access projection;
- the service was already explicitly attached to that Customer Workspace by the V0.14 provisioning process.

Entitlement states are:

- `active`,
- `suspended`,
- `expired`,
- `revoked`.

Expiry is normalized automatically. Revocation is immutable. Service entitlement is intentionally a **restriction/eligibility layer** only; it never overrides Contract, Site Manifest, role, policy, resource state, field policy, privacy or security.

## Business customer exposure modes

V0.16 implements the approved modes exactly as business policy values:

- `hidden`,
- `read_only`,
- `approval_required`,
- `direct_edit`.

The ordering is restrictive. A business policy can reduce the canonical maximum but cannot increase it.

For example, if the canonical maximum is `approval_required`, staff cannot configure `direct_edit`. If the canonical maximum is `read_only`, customer editing cannot be enabled by a Digital service or customer role.

## Publishing is independent

Editing and publishing are modeled independently.

Customer publishing modes are:

- `not_applicable`,
- `staff_only`,
- `approval_required`,
- `direct_publish`.

A customer may therefore be allowed to edit content while publishing remains staff-only, or customer publication may require an approval request.

`not_applicable` is protected as a canonical semantic. Staff cannot transform a canonically non-publishing capability into a publishing capability or redefine a publishing resource as non-publishing to bypass its rules.

## Field-level policy

A capability may contain mixed field exposure.

Field policies may reference only canonical fields from the trusted resolver projection. They cannot create arbitrary field paths.

A field's effective maximum is the most restrictive of:

- canonical capability maximum,
- canonical field maximum,
- staff customer capability policy,
- optional staff field override,
- active privacy/security restrictions.

A field override cannot exceed either its canonical field maximum or its parent customer capability policy.

## Customer role permissions

Customer business roles remain separate from Platform staff roles.

V0.16 introduces Site-scoped customer-role permission grants. A grant references:

- managed Site Connection,
- Customer Role,
- exact canonical Permission ID.

The permission must already be present in the V0.15 canonical validation evidence and must actually be referenced by the canonical customer access projection for that Site.

This prevents staff from typing or inventing broad role strings and treating them as authorization.

A customer role grant is only one layer. It cannot override Site support, service entitlement, exposure policy, field policy, resource state, privacy/security restrictions or workspace/membership state.

## Privacy and security restrictions

Privacy/security overlays are reduction-only.

They may apply to:

- an entire canonical capability, or
- one canonical field.

Allowed restriction modes are intentionally limited to:

- `hidden`,
- `read_only`.

A restriction requires an explicit reason and can never increase customer access.

## Effective authorization evaluator

`customerCapabilityPolicyStore.evaluate()` is the reusable policy decision foundation for the future Customer Workspace API.

The evaluator requires:

- active connected Site Connection with valid canonical evidence,
- active/read-only Customer Workspace,
- active accepted workspace membership,
- active customer role,
- current resolver projection matching current Site validation evidence,
- Site Manifest support for the capability,
- active service entitlement,
- explicit Customer Capability Access Policy,
- resource-state decision from the relevant domain/resource adapter,
- field policy when a canonical field is targeted,
- privacy/security restrictions,
- exact canonical role permission requirement.

Missing required information denies the operation.

### Read

Read is permitted only when the effective exposure is not hidden, the resource state permits read, and the exact canonical read-permission requirement is satisfied.

### Edit

- `hidden` → denied.
- `read_only` → denied.
- `approval_required` → allowed only through future `change_request` workflow.
- `direct_edit` → direct mutation path may be used by the future trusted API.

### Publish

Publishing also requires resource-state permission and exact canonical publishing permission.

- `not_applicable` → denied as not a publishing resource.
- `staff_only` → customer publishing denied.
- `approval_required` → future `publish_request` workflow.
- `direct_publish` → future trusted publishing API may publish directly.

A read-only effective exposure also blocks customer publishing regardless of publishing mode.

## Resource state is explicit

The policy engine does not invent generic canonical resource states because different Contract resources may have different lifecycle rules.

Instead the future resource adapter/API supplies a trusted `ResourceStateAuthorization` decision containing read/edit/publish booleans and an optional reason. The policy engine intersects that decision with every other authorization layer.

This keeps lifecycle logic with the authoritative resource implementation while still preventing it from bypassing customer authorization.

## Staff Website Platform UX

A new `Entitlements & Access Policy` view has been added to NEXT F Digital → Website Platform.

The staff surface shows:

- canonical customer access projection evidence,
- service capability entitlements,
- entitlement lifecycle actions,
- customer capability exposure policy,
- independent publishing policy,
- field-level restrictions,
- privacy/security restrictions,
- Site-scoped customer role → exact canonical Permission grants.

Configuration controls remain disabled when there is no current canonical customer access projection.

The UI explicitly states that the release seeds no invented Module, Capability, field or Permission IDs.

## Authorization boundary

This browser/local-store implementation is the product/domain model only. It is **not** the production security boundary.

When the shared backend/API phase is implemented, every customer operation must re-evaluate the same policy on the trusted server/API using authenticated principal, workspace/site/resource scope and current authoritative records.

The UI may hide or disable controls for usability, but browser state must never be trusted as authorization.

## Deliberately not implemented in V0.16

V0.16 does not implement:

- real Contract Registry adapter population;
- real Customer Workspace API;
- customer frontend screens;
- website content resource persistence;
- change-request persistence;
- review/approval queues;
- revisions/diffs;
- concurrency conflict handling;
- publish execution;
- public website delivery APIs;
- production D1/R2/Workers persistence.

Those belong to subsequent phases after this authorization foundation.

## Next phase

V0.17.0 should implement **Change Requests, Approvals, Revisions and Publishing Governance**.

Approval-required customer edits must remain separate from authoritative/published state, carry a base revision, detect stale/conflicting changes, and distinguish edit, submit-for-approval, approve and publish permissions.
