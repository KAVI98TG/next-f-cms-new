# NEXT F CMS Scope and Architecture Direction

NEXT F currently has four major business areas: **Platform, NEXT F Digital, Gaming Store, and NEXT F Software**. For the current phase of the CMS project, however, the scope is intentionally limited to **Platform and NEXT F Digital**. Gaming Store and NEXT F Software operate through their own separate websites and business systems and should be handled independently when their dedicated integration work is required. Gaming Store operates through **gaming.nextf.lk**, while NEXT F Software operates through **software.nextf.lk**. Both businesses may later consume appropriate shared Platform capabilities, but their business-specific websites, customer journeys, operational workflows, and CMS modules must not influence or complicate the NEXT F Digital architecture being designed in this phase.

**Platform** is the common foundation of the NEXT F ecosystem. It should contain capabilities that are shared across businesses rather than owned by one division. This includes platform users, staff identity, authentication, roles and permissions, organizations and workspaces, account security, shared notifications, audit infrastructure, system logs, global settings, integrations, system health, help-center infrastructure, common operational services, shared file and communication capabilities where appropriate, and other cross-business administrative functions. Platform should act as the central identity, governance, security, and operational foundation on which NEXT F Digital and future business systems can depend.

The Platform identity model should distinguish clearly between **identity and authorization**. Creating a NEXT F account establishes a person's identity, but it must not automatically grant access to a Customer Workspace, project, website, administrative system, or business capability. One NEXT F identity may eventually participate in different contexts through explicit memberships, roles, entitlements, and provisioning. This allows the same account to remain stable while its authorized access changes according to the person's relationship with NEXT F.

**NEXT F Digital** is the primary business being designed in this phase. The main public website, **nextf.lk**, represents NEXT F Digital and its services, customer acquisition journey, pricing, resources, projects, case studies, and other customer-facing business content. The NEXT F Digital area inside the internal CMS should manage the operational side of this business, including leads, contacts, customers, organizations, opportunities, proposals, contracts, services and packages, projects, project milestones, deliverables, approvals, websites, support, billing, invoices, payments, subscriptions, renewals, files, content, SEO activities, marketing operations, reports, and the other workflows required to operate NEXT F Digital.

The system must clearly separate the **internal Staff and Platform Admin experience** from the **Customer Workspace experience**. NEXT F staff, administrators, and future employees should use the private internal Admin CMS to manage Platform and NEXT F Digital operations. Customers must never be given access to this internal administrative interface. The Customer Workspace should be a separate frontend and user experience designed specifically for customers, even if the Admin CMS and Customer Workspace share controlled backend services, APIs, databases, event infrastructure, authentication components, or other Platform services.

A person must first register and create a **NEXT F Account** before becoming eligible for Customer Workspace access. Registration alone, however, must never automatically create an active customer workspace or grant access to managed websites. The customer lifecycle should distinguish concepts such as account, contact, customer organization, project, service relationship, workspace, and connected site instead of treating them as the same entity. A typical real-customer flow may therefore progress through an account becoming associated with a customer or organization, receiving an eligible NEXT F Digital service or project, having a workspace provisioned, and then having one or more approved websites, projects, or services connected to that workspace.

The tenancy model must not assume that one account represents one company or one website. A customer organization may contain multiple users, multiple projects, several websites, different services, and different permission levels. The architecture should therefore support relationships such as **Account to Organization to Workspace to Projects, Sites, and Services**. Workspace membership should be separate from staff Platform roles, and customer-facing roles should use their own permission model. Depending on the customer's needs, roles may include concepts such as Owner, Customer Admin, Content Editor, Marketing User, Billing Contact, Contributor, or Read Only. Customer administrators may eventually be allowed to invite colleagues, but those invitations must remain constrained by the organization's authorized capabilities and must never grant Platform or NEXT F staff permissions.

The Customer Workspace is intended primarily for customers whose websites or digital systems are built, implemented, or managed by NEXT F according to the standards and contracts defined through **https://contracts.nextf.lk/**. It should not be treated as a generic public CMS automatically available to everyone who registers at NEXT F. Access must be explicitly provisioned according to the customer's project, purchased or contracted service, implementation, organization, and relationship with NEXT F. NEXT F administrators must remain in control of whether a workspace is activated, which projects and sites are connected, which users belong to it, and which customer capabilities are enabled.

The Customer Workspace should support at least three fundamental account and access states. A person may have a registered NEXT F account with no Customer Workspace entitlement, an approved demo account with access only to an isolated demonstration environment, or an approved real customer account with authorized access to one or more real NEXT F Digital workspaces, projects, services, or sites. These states must not be represented merely as cosmetic UI differences. They should be enforced through proper provisioning, authorization, tenancy, entitlements, and backend access controls.

NEXT F should also be able to make the Customer Workspace publicly discoverable as a **product demonstration**. A prospective customer may learn about the workspace through the NEXT F website and submit a formal **Demo Access Request**. Demo access must never be granted automatically simply because someone registers an account. The request should enter the NEXT F Admin CMS, where authorized staff can review it and approve, reject, revoke, extend, or otherwise manage it according to the defined demo lifecycle.

The demo environment must remain completely separated from real customer workspaces and production customer data. Demo users should see only safe sample organizations, projects, websites, content, reports, approvals, files, SEO information, support examples, and other controlled demonstration data. Demo users must never gain access to real customer information, internal administrative functions, staff-only records, production credentials, private integrations, billing secrets, API secrets, unrestricted website controls, or production mutation paths. Where interactive functionality is useful, demo actions should operate only against disposable or simulated data.

Demo access should have its own lifecycle rather than behaving like a real workspace with a simple demo flag. Appropriate states may include request submitted, under review, approved, rejected, active, expired, revoked, or extended according to the final implementation. Demo access may have an expiry date, usage restrictions, disabled capabilities, resettable sample data, abuse protection, and controlled renewal rules. Converting a prospect into a real customer must require a separate production provisioning process. A demo workspace must never become a production customer workspace simply by changing one boolean field.

For real customers, the internal NEXT F Digital Admin CMS and the Customer Workspace should be tightly connected through the backend while remaining clearly separated in interface and authorization. When NEXT F staff update a customer's project, milestone, site, contract, invoice, subscription, support ticket, deliverable, SEO activity, documentation, approval request, or other customer-visible information in the Admin CMS, the appropriate information should become available in that customer's workspace from the same authoritative business data wherever possible.

The reverse direction is equally important. Customer actions performed through the Customer Workspace, such as approving a deliverable, requesting changes, uploading files, replying to a project update, submitting a support ticket, acknowledging a document, updating permitted business information, editing allowed website content, submitting SEO changes, or requesting publication should flow into the appropriate NEXT F Digital workflow. The Admin CMS and Customer Workspace should not maintain independent copies of the same business entities. Projects, invoices, contracts, support tickets, deliverables, subscriptions, sites, and other shared entities should have a defined system of record and be presented differently according to the viewer's role and permissions.

The same principle applies to website management. For websites built or managed by NEXT F, the Customer Workspace must operate according to the architecture and contracts defined by **contracts.nextf.lk**. That registry should remain the authoritative technical source for website schemas, modules, capabilities, APIs, permissions, events, webhooks, Site Manifests, Customer CMS metadata, Admin metadata, security requirements, compatibility rules, versioning, and other implementation standards. The Customer Workspace should consume those contracts rather than independently inventing website-management structures.

A NEXT F-built website should expose only the functionality deliberately supported by its Site Manifest and applicable contract version. Different websites may expose different modules and capabilities. One site may support pages, blogs, SEO, and media, while another may support only selected content and SEO capabilities. The presence of a technical capability does not automatically mean that every customer may use it. The Site Manifest defines what the website supports, while customer authorization determines what the particular customer may actually access.

A formal **Customer Capability Access Policy** should therefore sit between technical website capabilities and the Customer Workspace experience. This policy should define the maximum customer exposure for each resource and should reuse the existing Permissions, Site Manifest, Customer CMS metadata, Admin metadata, APIs, publishing contracts, and other systems defined through contracts.nextf.lk instead of creating a second authorization architecture. At a business level, customer-facing resources should be classifiable into controlled states such as **hidden, read only, direct edit, or approval required**.

A hidden resource should never be exposed to the Customer Workspace. A read-only resource may be displayed but not modified. A direct-edit resource may be changed by an authorized customer where the required permissions, scope, validation, and Site Manifest capability all allow it. An approval-required resource should allow the customer to propose a change without immediately replacing the authoritative or published state. The proposed change should enter an appropriate review workflow for NEXT F staff.

Editing and publishing should also be treated as separate capabilities. A customer may be permitted to edit content while publication still requires NEXT F approval. Other resources may allow direct publishing when the contract, project agreement, capability, permission, and validation rules permit it. Some resources may be editable by customers but publishable only by NEXT F staff. Resources that do not use publishing should explicitly indicate that publishing is not applicable.

The capability system should also support field-level restrictions. A resource may contain a mixture of customer-editable fields, customer-visible but read-only fields, approval-required fields, internal fields, and completely hidden or sensitive fields. For example, a customer may be allowed to update business contact information while internal identifiers, system metadata, deployment configuration, integration secrets, staff notes, and security data remain unavailable. Field-level rules must never weaken existing privacy or security classifications.

The effective customer authorization decision should be determined by the intersection of several layers rather than by one frontend setting. The canonical contract defines the maximum permitted exposure, the Site Manifest defines whether the website supports the capability, the customer or workspace entitlement determines whether the service includes that capability, the user's role and permissions determine whether that person may use it, the resource state determines whether the requested operation is valid, field policies determine what parts may be changed, and privacy or security restrictions may further reduce access. When these layers conflict, the most restrictive result should win.

Customer-facing UI must never become the actual authorization boundary. Hiding a button or removing a menu item is only a presentation decision. Every customer operation must be validated through backend authorization using the appropriate tenant, workspace, resource, role, permission, and scope. Missing or unresolved customer write policy should fail closed rather than granting access by assumption.

Where a customer action requires NEXT F approval, the system should use a proper **change request or approval workflow**. The customer should submit a proposed change against a known resource revision. That proposal should remain separate from the authoritative published state until reviewed. NEXT F staff should be able to approve it, reject it, request changes, or apply it according to the defined workflow. Approval history should record the requester, reviewer, decision, timestamps, resulting revision, and relevant audit information.

Version and concurrency handling are important for these workflows. A customer may submit a change while a staff member or another customer user modifies the same resource. Approval-required changes should therefore reference the version or revision they were based on. If the authoritative resource changes before the request is applied, the system should detect the stale proposal and send it through an appropriate conflict or review process rather than silently overwriting newer work.

The Customer Workspace should provide structured website management instead of unrestricted website administration. Depending on the customer's service and site capabilities, manageable resources may include approved page content, blogs, media, documentation, business information, SEO fields, forms, requests, or other specifically contracted resources. Highly sensitive areas such as deployment infrastructure, server configuration, credentials, secret keys, internal code, unrestricted HTML or scripting, staff-only notes, and protected operational settings should remain hidden or tightly controlled.

NEXT F should continue to build websites as professionally managed systems. The Customer Workspace is therefore not intended to become a generic website builder or hosting control panel. Its purpose is to give customers meaningful control over the parts of their digital presence they genuinely need while NEXT F retains responsibility for architecture, implementation quality, security, integrations, deployment, and other professionally managed areas.

The Customer Workspace also needs a clear distinction between **workspace data and connected website data**. Workspace data includes projects, proposals, contracts, invoices, subscriptions, support tickets, approvals, files, deliverables, notifications, project communications, and other operational information. Connected website data includes intentionally exposed content, SEO fields, forms, media, analytics summaries, deployment or health information, and other site-specific capabilities. The Admin CMS and Customer Workspace manage the business relationship, while contracts.nextf.lk defines how a connected NEXT F-built website safely exposes supported capabilities.

Website connections should have a controlled lifecycle. A site must be explicitly associated with the correct customer organization, workspace, project, and applicable contract version. The system should define how a site proves its identity, how connection credentials or service authorization are issued, how supported modules and capabilities are discovered through the Site Manifest, how contract upgrades are handled, and how access is revoked when the site is replaced, migrated, disconnected, or no longer managed by NEXT F. Secrets must never be placed directly inside public Site Manifests.

Customer Workspace access and functionality should also be influenced by **service entitlements**. Purchasing or being assigned a particular service may enable specific workspace or site-management capabilities, but service entitlements must not bypass authorization or contract restrictions. Entitlements may restrict access further, but they should not grant capabilities that the site's canonical contract marks unavailable or hidden.

Notifications and communications should be treated as shared Platform capabilities. Events occurring in NEXT F Digital or the Customer Workspace may generate appropriate in-app or email notifications. Examples include project updates, approval requests, customer change submissions, support replies, invoice issuance, payment confirmation, subscription renewal, deliverable availability, site health issues, demo-request decisions, and security-related events. Customer notification preferences can later control delivery channels without changing the underlying authoritative business event.

Important staff and customer actions should be auditable. This includes workspace provisioning, membership invitations, role changes, content changes, approval submissions, publishing, file actions, site connection changes, support activity, billing-related actions, demo approvals, contract acknowledgements, authentication-sensitive actions, and other important events. Internal staff audit records and customer-visible activity histories may originate from the same event infrastructure but should expose different levels of detail according to permissions and privacy requirements.

Customer lifecycle and offboarding rules also need to be part of the architecture. When a project completes, a service ends, a subscription expires, payment obligations are unresolved, the customer terminates the relationship, or NEXT F stops managing a site, the system should define what happens to Customer Workspace access and connected website capabilities. Depending on the business situation, the workspace may become read-only, certain capabilities may be disabled, exports may remain available for a defined period, website connections may be revoked, and internal records may remain retained according to NEXT F's operational and legal requirements.

Data ownership and portability should be explicitly respected. Customer organizations may own their website content, domains, hosting accounts, analytics properties, advertising accounts, Search Console properties, third-party business accounts, and other assets according to the project and applicable agreements. NEXT F may store authorized integration references, operational records, project information, contracts, audit data, and configuration needed to provide its services. The system should define what customers may export, what NEXT F retains after termination, how long records are kept, and which internal, security, or infrastructure information is never exposed.

The Customer Workspace should be deployed as a separate customer-facing application or frontend from the internal Admin CMS, even if they share APIs and Platform infrastructure. Its final hostname may be decided separately, but the architectural separation should remain clear. Staff authentication and customer authentication should also remain logically separated. The existing Cloudflare Access based administrative authentication model should not simply be reused as customer authentication without deliberate design. Customer identity requires its own registration, verification, session, recovery, workspace membership, entitlement, and authorization lifecycle.

The relationship between the Admin CMS and Customer Workspace should follow a strict source-of-truth principle. A resource should not become duplicated merely because both staff and customers can view or interact with it. The same project, invoice, contract, site, ticket, deliverable, or approval should normally remain one authoritative business entity, with visibility and available actions determined by the viewer's role, workspace membership, capability policy, and permissions.

The architecture should therefore be understood as a connected system with clear boundaries. **Platform provides the shared identity, security, governance, notifications, audit, and operational foundation. NEXT F Digital provides the primary business workflows. The internal Admin CMS is used exclusively by NEXT F staff to operate Platform and NEXT F Digital. The separate Customer Workspace provides controlled access to eligible NEXT F Digital customers and approved demo users. NEXT F-built websites connect to that environment through versioned contracts, Site Manifests, APIs, permissions, events, and capability policies governed by contracts.nextf.lk.**

Whenever authoritative information is required about website architecture, schemas, modules, capabilities, customer access policies, permissions, APIs, events, webhooks, Site Manifests, security rules, compatibility, versioning, Customer CMS metadata, Admin metadata, or implementation standards, **https://contracts.nextf.lk/** should be treated as the primary technical source of truth. Business-level Customer Workspace decisions should be designed so that they reference and extend those contracts rather than contradicting or duplicating them.

From this point forward, unless a task explicitly says otherwise, work in this conversation should focus on **Platform, NEXT F Digital, the internal Admin CMS, the separate Customer Workspace, account and workspace provisioning, organizations and customer roles, Demo Access, customer capability and approval policies, NEXT F-managed website integration, synchronization between staff and customer workflows, notifications, auditability, lifecycle and offboarding, and the contracts.nextf.lk standards that govern those systems**. Gaming Store and NEXT F Software should remain outside the current design scope except where they later consume genuinely shared Platform capabilities.
